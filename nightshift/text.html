The motel ice machine is on line two

Night clerk’s statement

Night clerk Mae Vale shut the lobby at midnight. She put the phone in the office safe after a caller tried to book all eight rooms in the name of his dog. At 2:13, KDAR’s incoming line displayed the motel number. Clem picked it up. On tape, something clattered against galvanized steel.

“I had the night key and an empty bucket. When I put the bucket down, the ice machine quit rattling. When I moved it, Clem said he could hear me again. I’ve given people wake-up calls for eleven years. This was my first one from an appliance.”
— Mae Vale, night clerk

The night desk checks its work

Engineer Len Rusk visited the vending alcove carrying a continuity tester, a pocket radio and a lemon-lime soda. The machine has one power cord, one water line and a label that says GUESTS ONLY. Len rented Room 3 so he could continue the test.

The green tube over the machine was on its own circuit. Switching off the amber porch bulb made no difference to the line. Len’s photograph shows both lights on. The red dot over the fence is our tower.

The first two rings on our reel came through the board before Clem brought up the channel. According to Len, a compressor can throw noise back down a power line. It would still have to know our area code.

The Saturday log

Host Clem Orr takes the overnight shift. Len Rusk has the transmitter keys. All times are Pacific.

Program order

  • 12:00 AMDry lake weather. Clem reads the gauge outside the pump house. The needle has pointed to “fair” since the roof came off.
  • 01:00 AMLost & found frequency. A baker hears a polka in his flour sifter. Len demonstrates what a loose belt sounds like. The baker prefers the polka.
  • 02:13 AMThe motel ice machine. Mae Vale calls in from the Blue Moon. Play the aircheck.
  • 03:00 AMWrong-number hour. Clem calls back the people who asked for the radiator shop. Tonight’s topic is a 1973 station wagon that only overheats on birthdays.

A correction from Friday

The appliance making three descending tones on the faxed report was a bread machine. We owe the entire electric blanket community a correction. Len has amended the station log in pencil.

Reception desk

October signal reports

Oct 16 · P. Bell, Cold Storage Road. “You came in clear as anything in the walk-in freezer. Stepped into the kitchen and lost you completely.” Len thinks the freezer door is acting as a reflector. Clem would like a report from the dairy aisle.

Oct 15 · T. Gray, Route 8. “Reception dropped every time I passed the billboard with the giant soup can.” The engineer has entered this into the obstructions register under vegetables.

Oct 14 · M. Vale, Blue Moon Motor Lodge. “Signal fine. Your clock runs four minutes slow.” Mae runs the night desk at the Blue Moon. She sets her watch by a truck that turns up at 4:17 every morning, including Sundays.
